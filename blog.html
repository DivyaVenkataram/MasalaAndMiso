  • New York · 3 Michelin stars · March 2025

    Le Bernardin: The Ocean on a Plate

    Eric Ripert's temple of seafood remains the gold standard.

    What defines Le Bernardin isn't excess—it's clarity. Every course speaks in a single, precise dialect: the language of the sea. The langoustine with sea urchin and caviar is a masterclass in umami layering; the barely cooked fish preparations remind you why the kitchen has held three stars for decades. Service is seamless, the room understated. This is fine dining as meditation.

    Verdict: Essential. The omakase redefines restraint. Book well ahead.

  • Chicago · 3 Michelin stars · February 2025

    Alinea: Twenty Years of Edible Theatre

    Grant Achatz's flagship still delivers the most ambitious tasting menu in America.

    Alinea doesn't just serve food—it composes it. From the iconic balloon to the table-side narratives, every moment is choreographed. The technical execution is flawless: textures, temperatures, and flavors are deployed like vocabulary. Some dishes shock; others soothe. The gallery menu is long, but the pacing never drags. This is culinary language at its most articulate.

    Verdict: A pilgrimage for anyone who cares about where dining can go. Save for the full experience.

  • Tokyo · 3 Michelin stars · January 2025

    Jiro: The Last Word in Edomae

    Growing up in Tokyo, I learned that shari and neta are a dialogue. Here, that dialogue is perfect.

    Sukiyabashi Jiro needs no introduction. The shari—warm, seasoned with red vinegar—and the neta are in perfect balance. Each piece is a sentence in the story of Edomae sushi. The rice is the secret: body, temperature, and acidity calibrated over a lifetime. The experience is brief, intense, and unrepeatable. Reservations are famously difficult; the meal is worth every effort.

    Verdict: The definitive Edomae experience. Go with reverence and an empty stomach.

  • San Francisco · 3 Michelin stars · December 2024

    Benu: East Meets West in the Tenderloin

    Corey Lee's multi-cultural tasting menu is both precise and soulful.

    Benu threads Korean, Chinese, and French technique into a single narrative. The thousand-year-old quail egg, the faux shark's fin, the lobster coral xiao long bao—each dish is a thesis. The dining room is serene; the service exacting. This is where technical rigor meets emotional depth. One of America's most important restaurants.

    Verdict: A must for anyone interested in the future of fine dining. Book the chef's counter if you can.

  • Paris · 3 Michelin stars · November 2024

    Arpège: Alain Passard's Vegetable Revolution

    The garden is the star. Meat is optional; vegetables are the main event.

    At Arpège, the produce from Passard's gardens drives the menu. Beets, carrots, and heirloom tomatoes are elevated to the level of luxury ingredients. The famous œuf à la coque with maple syrup and vinegar is still transcendent. The room is classic; the cooking is anything but. A landmark of modern French cuisine.

    Verdict: Essential Paris. Request a table with a view of the kitchen when you reserve.
